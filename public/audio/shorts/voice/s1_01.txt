At three hundred fifty kilometers per hour, a bullet train turns a tunnel into a cannon. The air ahead cannot escape through the narrow gap. It compresses into a moving wall, then fires out of the far portal. Four hundred meters away, windows rattle and glass can crack. The train never touches anything. The air does all of it. Engineers call this tunnel boom. The obvious fix is a sharper nose, but a needle shape adds weight and crosswind instability. The real fix came from a bird that dives without a splash. A beak solved a pressure problem.
