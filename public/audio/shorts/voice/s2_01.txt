Inside a long tunnel, the pressure ahead of a fast train can climb to several kilopascals. That is enough force to flex steel and shake concrete. The pressure wave steepens as it runs, then reaches the portal and radiates into open air. Low frequencies bend around buildings, so the boom arrives hundreds of meters away. Sound crosses that distance in about one second. Residents hear a thud, then a crack. The engine is innocent. The tunnel mouth is the weapon. The pressure does not need a crash to cause damage. It only needs a fast train and a narrow tube.
